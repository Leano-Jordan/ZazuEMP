## 2. Copyright

South African copyright protection covers eligible original works, including computer programs. CIPC states that most eligible copyright works do not require registration and that protection is secured automatically when the legal requirements are met.

The Copyright Act provides a general author-ownership rule, subject to statutory exceptions and agreements. Employment, commissioned works, joint authorship and third-party contributions therefore require separate factual review.

Zazu action:
- Keep Isaac identified as current owner of original solo-developed project material.
- Keep proprietary copyright notices in the repository.
- Preserve Git history and dated artifacts.
- Do not claim ownership over third-party components.
- Obtain written contributor/contractor terms before accepting material where ownership matters.

## 4. Trade marks and brand

Zazu, Zazu EMP, logos, slogans and distinctive visual identity are separate from copyright.

CIPC states that registered trade marks receive statutory protection under the Trade Marks Act, while unregistered marks may have common-law protection.

Before substantial commercial investment in the brand:
- search the relevant trade-mark registers;
- assess confusingly similar marks;
- decide which classes and territories matter;
- consider trade-mark registration;
- keep evidence of first use and brand creation.

## 5. Third-party software and assets

The proprietary Zazu notice does not replace third-party licences.

For every dependency or external asset that materially enters a release:
- identify the exact version;
- identify the licence;
- preserve the licence text where required;
- record required notices/attribution;
- check whether the licence permits the intended commercial use and distribution model.

The current repository manifests show the application using Laravel/PHP and Vite/Tailwind tooling. A full dependency-level licence audit remains a release activity.

## 6. Personal information and POPIA

Zazu processes or is designed to process names, contact details and identifiable photos. Before real production use, the actual responsible-party/operator arrangement must be documented.

Engineering gates:
- authentication and session security;
- business membership and server-side data isolation;
- roles and permissions;
- controlled media access;
- purpose limitation and data minimisation;
- retention/deletion rules;
- incident response;
- operator agreements where third parties process information;
- backup and recovery controls;
- data-subject access/correction/deletion handling where applicable.

POPIA section 19 requires appropriate, reasonable technical and organisational measures and regular verification and updating of safeguards. Section 21 addresses written contracts with operators that process personal information on behalf of a responsible party. Section 22 addresses security-compromise notification.

## 7. PAIA, ECTA and CPA

These laws may become relevant depending on how Zazu is offered and operated:

- **PAIA:** access to records held by private bodies and related obligations.
- **ECTA:** electronic communications, data messages and electronic transactions.
- **CPA:** consumer-facing goods/services, agreements, disclosures and unfair business practices.

Actual applicability must be mapped to the eventual commercial model, contracts and customer types before launch.

## 8. Photography and event media

Photography and camera-hire capabilities are now part of the supported event-service direction.

If Zazu later stores event photographs, videos, identity images or other event media:
- treat identifiable media as personal information where applicable;
- define the business purpose;
- control access by authorised business users;
- preserve consent/contract evidence where required;
- define retention and deletion;
- avoid exposing media through public URLs by default;
- address photographer/customer ownership and usage rights separately from the software's ownership.

The fact that Zazu manages a photography service does not give Zazu ownership of photographs created by a customer, photographer or contractor.
