## 1. What Zazu is

Zazu EMP is an event-management application used by businesses to organise customers, events/jobs, services, purchasing, inventory/assets, documents, invoices, payments, reports and related operational records.

## 2. Information Zazu may process

Depending on the features a business chooses to use, Zazu may process:

- account information such as name, email address and authentication/session information;
- business/workspace information;
- customer and contact information such as names, telephone numbers and email addresses;
- event/job information and notes;
- documents, receipts and other attachments intentionally uploaded by an authorised business user;
- profile and business-branding images;
- payment and invoice records entered into Zazu for business administration;
- audit and security records needed to operate and protect the service;
- technical information generated by the application, such as request/error records where applicable.

Zazu should not be described as collecting information that the deployed feature set does not actually collect.

## 3. Why information is used

The deployed Zazu service uses information for purposes such as:

- providing the requested event/business-management functionality;
- maintaining account and workspace access;
- keeping customer and job records connected to business operations;
- generating invoices, reports and operational records;
- protecting the service, detecting faults and investigating security events;
- maintaining backup and recovery capability;
- meeting applicable legal, regulatory and contractual obligations.

Any secondary purpose must be separately assessed before it is introduced.

## 4. Business responsibility and role allocation

For customer information entered by a Zazu customer, the final commercial arrangement must state clearly who acts as the responsible party and when Zazu/Rosscore Labs or another service provider acts as an operator/service provider.

The deployed contract must not rely on this draft to silently assign legal roles.

## 5. Data minimisation

Businesses should enter only information needed for a legitimate business purpose. Optional fields should remain optional unless the workflow genuinely requires them.

Zazu's engineering controls treat customer names, contact details and identifiable images as sensitive business data and restrict private media to authorised application access.

## 6. Where information is stored

The deployment may use local application storage, database storage and private media storage depending on configuration.

The final notice must be updated to state the actual deployment topology, any hosted infrastructure, any external operators and any cross-border transfers that actually occur.

## 7. Access and security

Zazu uses authentication, business-context controls, role/permission checks, private media delivery and audit/security records. These are technical safeguards; they do not replace an organisation's legal, contractual or administrative responsibilities.

## 8. Retention

Information is not deleted merely because an application record is soft-deleted.

The final deployment must follow the approved retention and disposal schedule in `RELEASE_RETENTION_DISPOSAL_SCHEDULE.md`, including any longer retention required by law, accounting, contractual or evidentiary needs.

## 9. Data-subject rights and requests

The final responsible-party process must explain how a person can request applicable access, correction/deletion or objection handling.

The operational procedure is recorded in `RELEASE_DATA_SUBJECT_REQUEST_PROCEDURE.md`.

## 10. Security compromises

The responsible-party incident process must address containment, investigation, escalation and applicable regulatory/data-subject notification.

The operating procedure is recorded in `RELEASE_SECURITY_INCIDENT_RESPONSE.md`.

## 11. Operators and third parties

See `RELEASE_OPERATOR_DATA_PROCESSING_CONTROL.md`.
